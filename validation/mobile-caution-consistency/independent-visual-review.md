## Most Important Interpretation Pitfalls

1. **Exclude the followed user's identity.** The large central person box around the black-shirt user must not trigger a generic “pedestrian blocking ahead” alert, but this does not justify excluding every pedestrian in the image.
2. **The user and camera pass objects at different times.** The blue-shirt and green-shirt pedestrians, cone, and planters remain in the camera foreground after the user passes. Single-frame rules based on image size or center can produce delayed alerts.
3. **Image left/center/right is not the ground travel corridor.** The followed user, nearby columns, and already-passed pedestrians can overlap the image center. Foot/ground-contact positions, user trajectory, and frame history must be considered together.
4. **Visible does not mean blocking.** The blue-shirt curbside pedestrian, right-side trash cans, seated person, sign, road vehicles, and bench inside the colonnade should be assessed separately from dynamic near-route pedestrians and fixed objects constraining the corridor.
5. **Route constraints often involve an object pair.** At 27–31 seconds, consider more than the right cone: if the system instructs a leftward avoidance maneuver, it must also consider the left planter. Left planters with right-side columns/building boundaries recur in the second half.
6. **No metric ground truth is available.** This visual review did not calibrate the camera, camera/user positions, depth, human scale, or ground geometry, and cannot provide reliable meters, TTC, stop thresholds, or “safe passage” certification.
7. **A closed cover is not a hole.** Ground covers and color differences in brick paving may be segmentation targets, but there is no evidence of an open pothole in this video.
